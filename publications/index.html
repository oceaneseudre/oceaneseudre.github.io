2023

Martín-Zamora, F. M., Liang, Y., Seudre, O., & Martín-Durán, J. M., et al (2023). Annelid functional genomics reveal the origins of bilaterian life cycles. Nature, 615(7950), 105-110.

We compare larval and adult developmental programmes in Owenia fusiformis to argue that temporal decoupling between head and trunk development helped shape the evolution of bilaterian life cycles.

2022

Seudre, O., Carrillo-Baltodano, A. M., Liang, Y., & Martín-Durán, J. M. (2022). ERK1/2 is an ancestral organising signal in spiral cleavage. Nature communications, 13(1), 2286.

I show that ERK1/2 activity marks the D-quadrant organiser in a basal annelid and acts as an ancestral signalling mechanism for posterior, mesodermal, and gut patterning in spiralians.

Seudre, O., Martín-Zamora, F. M., Rapisarda, V., Luqman, I., Carrillo-Baltodano, A. M., & Martín-Durán, J. M. (2022). The Fox gene repertoire in the annelid Owenia fusiformis reveals multiple expansions of the foxQ2 class in Spiralia. Genome Biology and Evolution, 14(10), evac139.

I reconstruct the Fox gene repertoire of Owenia fusiformis and show that repeated expansion of the foxQ2 class accompanied diversification of developmental roles in Spiralia.

2021

Carrillo-Baltodano, A. M., Seudre, O., Guynes, K., & Martín-Durán, J. M. (2021). Early embryogenesis and organogenesis in the annelid Owenia fusiformis. EvoDevo, 12(1), 5.

We establish a detailed staging framework for early embryogenesis and larval organogenesis in Owenia fusiformis to support comparative spiralian evo-devo.

2020

Seudre, O., Vanhoenacker, E., Mauger, S., Coudret, J., & Roze, D. (2020). Genetic variability and transgenerational regulation of investment in sex in the monogonont rotifer Brachionus plicatilis. Journal of Evolutionary Biology, 33(1), 112-120.

I show that investment in sex in the rotifer Brachionus plicatilis varies genetically and is shaped by both current conditions and transgenerational effects.

2018

Seudre, O., Namias, A., Gardella, O., Da Silva, G., Gouyon, P. H., & López-Villavicencio, M. (2018). Why outcross? The abandon-ship hypothesis in a facultative outcrossing/selfing fungal species. Fungal Genetics and Biology, 120, 1-8.

I test the abandon-ship hypothesis in a facultatively outcrossing fungus and examine how the genetic benefits of outcrossing balance the costs of sex and selfing.
